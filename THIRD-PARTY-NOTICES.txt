BrowserAI - third-party notices
===============================

BrowserAI redistributes the components below, and these obligations attach at
first installer handoff, independently of the licence BrowserAI itself carries.
This file ships inside the installed artifact, beside BrowserAI.exe.

Where each notice lives inside the artifact
-------------------------------------------

  Node.js                 payload\node\LICENSE
  @playwright/mcp         payload\mcp\node_modules\@playwright\mcp\LICENSE
  playwright-core         payload\mcp\node_modules\playwright-core\LICENSE
                          payload\mcp\node_modules\playwright-core\NOTICE
                          payload\mcp\node_modules\playwright-core\ThirdPartyNotices.txt
  Velopack                this file, below
  ModelContextProtocol    this file, below
  Microsoft.Extensions.*  this file, below

Node's LICENSE ships whole because it aggregates the OpenSSL, ICU, V8, zlib and
c-ares terms; "a single node.exe, nothing else" drops them, and it is not
optional. The vendored node_modules tree ships intact, which is what satisfies
Apache-2.0 section 4 for the two Playwright packages.

The last three have no file of their own anywhere in this artifact. They are
NuGet packages compiled into BrowserAI.exe, and a NuGet package's licence stays
in the machine's package cache: it is never copied to a publish output, so
nothing carries it to a recipient unless it is reproduced here. MIT requires its
notice to travel with the binary and Apache-2.0 section 4(a) requires a copy of
the licence to reach every recipient, so all three are reproduced below in full.

No browser is redistributed. Chromium, Firefox, ffmpeg and winldd are downloaded
to the user's machine on first run and no copy of any of them ships here. That
is the reason for first-run provisioning rather than a side benefit of it.
