## Toast popup measurement, 2026-10-08 22:45Z to 23:48Z: results

**Answer:** lane UI's design holds up. Removing the old toast and showing the new one under its own tag put a banner on screen **85 of 86 times** while the old banner was still showing. A first show managed 111 of 112, so that is as good as Windows gets. Replacing a toast under the same tag managed only **58 of 77** (p = 3.0e-6, Fisher exact). "A banner every time" is close but not exact: there was one miss in 86, and the toast events don't report it.

I ran 112 rounds with 300 shows. No round was aborted, no input arrived, and the screen never locked. I could not write `FINDINGS.md`, because sub-agents are told not to write findings `.md` files. Everything is below so you can save it there.

**How a banner was detected:**
- **Pixels:** the bottom-right corner of the screen above the taskbar was sampled every 40 ms and compared with a baseline frame taken after 2 s of stillness. A banner changed 50,165 to 99,316 pixels; a miss changed 0 or 1.
- **Image:** a crop of that corner was saved 1.5 s after each show. I checked the contact sheets by eye.
- **Sound:** the peak meter of the system-sounds audio session. Every one of the 271 banners sounded (median 124 ms after `Show` returned, range 64 to 1135 ms), and none of the 29 misses did.

**Scenario 4 in detail:**
- The quiet toast was in the Notification Centre all 8 times. That comes from the History API only; I did not open the Notification Centre.
- 40 of 40 countdown updates on the quiet toast succeeded, and no banner appeared.
- The event hand recorded `Dismissed(TimedOut)` 27 to 28 ms after the quiet show, 2 of 2.

**Notification Centre state:** after every show the history held exactly that round's one toast, and 0 other toasts under the app id. Every round's cleanup left 0 behind.

### Why same-tag replacements go silent

What it correlates with:
- **The replaced banner was on screen:** 19 of 77 silent. When the previous replacement had already been silent, so nothing was showing, 15 of 15 popped.
- **How long that banner had been up:** 5 of 10 silent after 60 s, against 14 of 67 after 3 s. That difference (p = 0.11) is suggestive only.

What does not explain it:
- **Input:** there was none all night. The 2026-10-08 daytime run, with him at the machine, had 2 of 5 silent.
- **Mouse position:** the cursor never moved from (1837,2017), far from the banner.
- **Timing:** silent and popped replacements came at the same 3.0 to 3.04 s.
- **A slow call:** silent calls took 2 to 11 ms.
- **The Notification Centre:** it always held one toast, replaced in place.

**No event reports a silent replacement.** The event hand saw zero Dismissed, Failed or Activated events across 7 silent same-tag replacements (and 83 other shows), while the quiet positive control fired as expected. So a missed banner cannot be caught and raised again from those events.

The two misses with separate tags:
- **The first-show miss:** its `Show` call took 6016 ms, against a median of 5.5 ms, and the removals just before it were slow too (184 and 82 ms).
- **The installing miss:** 3.1 s after its ready banner appeared, with removals of about 30 ms each and a 9 ms show.

Both happened before the event hand existed, so whether they raise an event is not measured.

### Conditions, checked before every round (112 of 112)
- **Away:** no input for 1021 to 4777 s at round start, and 5051 s at 23:52Z. The idle counter never reset during the run.
- **Unlocked:** WTS session flags read unlocked and the input desktop was `Default`.
- **Nothing suppressing banners:**
  - `SHQueryUserNotificationState` read 5 (accepts notifications).
  - The quiet-hours profile read 0 (unrestricted).
  - No focus session was active and the notifier setting was `Enabled`.
  - The per-app registry key held no banner-off values.
- **Nothing of the rig on screen:** every process was started windowless.

### What it means for lane UI, and what I'd change
1. **Keep the design and record the measurement (recommended).** Replace the remark in `UpdateToastContent.cs` that says "pops up every time is the reading, not a measurement" with 85 of 86 (plus 2 of 2 with no banner showing), and the first-show rate of 111 of 112. Add it to `kb/windows/notifications.md` beside the old 4-of-6 entry.
2. **Same tag for all toasts:** rejected; measured at 58 of 77.
3. **Detect a missed banner and raise again:** there is no event to detect it with, so not recommended.
4. **Always raise twice:** that would double the banner and the chime about 99% of the time. Rejected.

The product's real case, a ready banner up for about 10 minutes before Installing replaces it, is measured only up to 60 s (10 of 10).

### Cleanup proof
- **Toasts:** each round removed its own group, never `Clear(appId)`. The history for Windows PowerShell's app id read 0 at 23:49:01Z, as it did before the run.
- **Processes:** I checked all 140 processes I recorded, plus 4 from the dry runs: none is still running. Four of those pids now belong to later processes (chrome, conhost twice, sleep), confirmed by start time; I didn't touch them.
- **Registry:** the Notifications key was exported before and after. The only differences are Windows' own counters for the PowerShell app:
  - `PeriodicNotificationCount` went from 40 to 392.
  - `LastNotificationAddedTime` went from 14:05:32Z to 23:47:51Z.
  - `IsOptOutCandidate` is still 0.

  `PushNotifications` and `Classes\AppUserModelId` did not change. The rig created no registry value and no shortcut.
- **Start Menu:** `BrowserAI (suite).lnk` disappeared during the run. The rig didn't touch it. Lane arch holds the suite lock (ordinary gate since 23:42Z), so its suite probably removed it; I did not verify that.
- **Screen captures:** the one full-corner capture and the dry-run shots were deleted after reading. What's kept is 219 crops of the strip where banners appear (wallpaper plus our toasts only; the banner position matched in every show) and 14 contact sheets. I did not take the suite lock; nothing needed it.

### Decisions and deviations
- **Many more rounds than "about six":** 112 rounds, which played about 271 chimes on the default speaker (volume 0.50) between 22:45Z and 23:48Z. The brief said "at least 6", and the extra rounds are what settled the comparison. I dropped a prepared 300 s batch to avoid more chimes.
- **Group names:** each round used its own group, `browserai-tp-<time>-rNN`, with the product's tags, so no other toast under PowerShell's app id could be touched.
- **Event hand:** I built a small .NET 10 process (`evhand`) to record toast events; the last two batches used it.
- **Overwritten crops:** crop file names repeated across batches, so later batches overwrote earlier full-size crops. The contact sheets keep every shot at half size.
- **Countdown timing:** the first countdown update came at about 1.5 s instead of 1 s.
